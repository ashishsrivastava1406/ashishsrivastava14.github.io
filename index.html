Case studies from seven years of consumer product work.

I'm a senior product manager at Flutter Entertainment, working on consumer growth, monetisation and platform products across India, the UK and Ireland. Each case study focuses on the constraint, the decision I took, the alternatives I considered, and what the measurement showed — including the times it went against the decision.

Scope

Category

I own a consumer category that has grown roughly 20x in monthly gross profit over three years, driven by repeat purchase rather than acquisition spend.

Platform

I set the vision for the multitenant platform that runs four consumer brands across India, the UK and Ireland from one codebase, and own the Junglee Rummy admin panel end to end, including its access management.

Monetisation

After India's real money gaming ban removed the existing revenue model, I chose and sequenced the four models that replaced it: virtual currency, rewarded advertising, in-app purchases and the platform's first subscription.

Leadership

I manage four product managers and two product analysts, and own the quarterly roadmap, prioritisation and experiment design across the portfolio.

How I work

Five things the case studies below have taught me, rather than five things I believe in general.

Instrument before intervening

The tournament lobby was described as dated. The loss was in the sort and category tab logic. Complaints report an experience rather than diagnose it, and the gap between what people say is wrong and what the funnel says is wrong is usually where the work is.

Start from the constraint

Regulation, inventory that expires, prize pools that run at a loss, and operations teams who have to configure the thing at four in the afternoon are not context around the product problem. They are the product problem, and a solution that ignores them is a solution to a different question.

Make the experiment able to disprove you

The lobby test ran three paths rather than two so the expensive half of the build could be measured on its own. It showed that half contributed nothing to the primary metric. An experiment that can only confirm the team's preferred answer is a launch with extra steps.

Measure what a user stopped doing

Every success metric on the lobby work pointed at tournaments. None measured what a player gave up to play one, and the substitution only surfaced because ring rake happened to sit on the guardrail list. Inside a product with competing surfaces, that belongs in the primary metric set.

Volume and value are different problems

Three separate experiments moved revenue per user and left frequency flat. The one that moved participation did it by leaving the surface entirely and meeting players where they already were. Recognising which of the two a piece of work can actually move prevents claiming the wrong win.

About

The arc

I started in risk at Amazon, investigating how people abuse a marketplace. That taught me to read behaviour from data before believing a story about it.

Platforms came next, at Affle and ABP News, where the product was infrastructure and the users were internal. Then Cars24, where the decision was a credit policy rather than a screen. Since 2022 I have been at Flutter, running a consumer category and the platform four brands sit on, and managing a team.

The through line is that each move brought the decisions closer to me: from detecting what happened, to building what others operated, to choosing what gets built and being answerable for whether it worked.
