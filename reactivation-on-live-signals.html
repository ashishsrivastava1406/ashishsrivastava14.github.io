Reactivation on Live Signals

Replacing scheduled ticket giveaways with incentives triggered by observed drop-off, held back long enough not to pay players who were converting anyway, and paired with the recall system that stopped granted tickets going unused.

On this page

  1. The problem
  2. What I decided, and what I turned down
  3. What happened
  4. What I learned

The problem

Free tournament tickets were already being given away. Players told us, repeatedly, that the system around them did not work:

  • I don't get enough free tickets
  • I don't know when I have received a ticket
  • I don't remember if I hold one
  • I don't know how to use it
  • Nothing reminds me before it expires

Two separate failures sit in that list. Tickets were being granted at moments that had nothing to do with what a player was doing, and tickets that were granted went unnoticed and unused. Incentive spend was leaving the business without changing behaviour at either end.

What I decided, and what I turned down

Trigger on an observed drop-off rather than on a schedule. Six points: abandoning a tournament join, leaving a details page without joining, abandoning a ticket purchase, a net-negative streak in ring games, winning a first practice game without ever playing for cash, and entering the add-cash page as a non-depositor and leaving. Each is a moment where a player has already shown intent and then stopped.

Wait before granting. The system holds the ticket for a configurable interval after the drop-off rather than firing immediately, so a player who would have completed the action on their own is not paid for doing it. This is the decision I would defend hardest. An incentive that fires the instant someone hesitates buys conversions that were already coming, and the faster the trigger, the worse the incrementality.

Cap it in four directions. How many qualifying actions in what window make a player eligible, how many gratifications they can see in a day, and how many tickets they can receive over a period. Configurable rather than fixed, because the right ceiling is an empirical question. Without caps a drop-off trigger teaches players to drop off.

Never stack. If the player already holds a discounted ticket for that tournament, nothing fires.

Escalate price only on the purchase page. A player who abandons a ticket on sale at one discount sees a deeper one. That is deliberate price discrimination against demonstrated hesitation, confined to the one surface where the player had already decided to buy.

Treat recall as a separate product. A ticket the player forgets is spend with no chance of return. The animation on receipt, the reward store icon carrying an unused-ticket state, and expiry nudges at one day, one hour and five minutes — with priority to the earliest-expiring ticket and no duplicate reminders — exist because granting more tickets would not have fixed the second half of the complaint list.

What happened

Read at D14 with matured retention.

The mechanism was adopted. Just over 3% of cash playing players joined a tournament using a gratification ticket, from a base of zero.

The primary metric moved modestly. Tournament lobby to cash tournament join success rose 1.33 points on unique users, though it fell slightly on clicks — more players succeeded, taking more attempts to do it.

The commercial result is weaker than it first appears, and this is the important part. Before outlier treatment, ARPU was up 4.13% and tournament rake per user up 5.45%. After outlier treatment, ARPU is up 0.22% and tournament rake per user is down 1.57%. Only ring rake and ARPU reached statistical significance at all, at +1.80% and +0.22% on the treated view. A metric that changes sign under outlier treatment is not a result.

Retention is the most durable effect. Among new depositors, tournament retention was positive at every read and strongest late, up 1.50 points at D7 and 1.15 at D14. Platform retention was positive throughout. Ring retention was mixed.

Ring rake rose more reliably than tournament rake. The statistically significant, outlier-robust movement came from the ring product, not the one the tickets were for.

What I learned

The honest read is small and real rather than large and fragile. Roughly 3% adoption, a point and a third on join success, a point and a half on tournament retention at D7, and an ARPU effect that survives scrutiny at 0.22%. Reporting the untreated 4.13% would have been defensible and wrong.

Delaying the incentive is what makes the number believable. Because the trigger waits, the players who converted are less likely to be ones who would have converted anyway. That design choice constrains the size of the win, and it is the reason the win can be attributed at all.

Distribution was never the constraint. Half the complaints were about not receiving tickets and half about not knowing what to do with the ones already received. Building only the first half would have increased spend and moved nothing.
